## Two Indices, Two Exchanges — One Pattern?

Seasonal patterns in financial markets are well documented: winter delivers better returns than summer, September is statistically the weakest month, and prices rise above average around the turn of the month.

But do these patterns apply **equally strongly** to the DAX and the S&P 500? Or are there differences investors should know about?

We analysed 35 years of daily data for both indices — XETRA data for the DAX (from 1991) and NYSE data for the S&P 500. All returns are normalised: each year starts at 100, and daily returns compound from there.

## The Annual Cycle in Direct Comparison

Looking at the average annual cycle for both indices, one thing stands out immediately: **the basic structure is similar, but the DAX shows larger swings.**

Both indices rise in January, go through a weaker phase in summer (May–September), and gain ground in the fourth quarter. The classic "Sell in May" structure is visible in both markets.

The key difference: **the DAX is more seasonal than the S&P 500.** The gap between the best and worst months is wider in the DAX. This means seasonal strategies potentially offer more return in the DAX — but also more risk.

**Three notable observations:**

1. **The DAX summer is significantly weaker.** June, August and September are noticeably more negative in the DAX than in the S&P 500. The reason: German investors actually go on holiday (lower liquidity), and European institutional investors reduce positions more aggressively than their US counterparts.

2. **The DAX winter is correspondingly stronger.** From October through April, the DAX beats the S&P in almost every month. The "Best Six Months" effect is more pronounced in the DAX.

3. **April is the standout month in the DAX.** With an average of +2.1%, April is the strongest month in the DAX — well ahead of the S&P 500 (+1.5%). This is partly due to dividend season: many DAX companies pay out in April/May, which generates buying pressure beforehand.

## Why the DAX Is More Seasonal

Three structural reasons explain the stronger seasonality in the DAX:

**1. Lower market depth**
The S&P 500 is the most liquid stock market in the world. Seasonal patterns get arbitraged away faster there. The DAX has fewer participants and lower volume — patterns persist longer.

**2. Export dependence**
40% of DAX revenues come from exports. Seasonal business cycles (capital goods orders in spring, summer slowdown) feed directly through to corporate earnings.

**3. Holiday calendar**
XETRA has different public holidays than the NYSE. Easter, Whitsun, and 3 October create trading pauses that do not exist in the US. Distinct patterns form around these days. The [holiday analysis on SeasonAlpha](https://seasonalpha.ai/feiertage) shows these effects in detail.

## Conclusion

**The DAX is the more seasonal index** — with stronger summer losses but also stronger winter gains. Seasonal strategies (above all "Sell in May") have worked better historically in the DAX than in the S&P 500.

This does not mean the DAX is "better." It means: **anyone trading seasonally should not ignore the DAX.** And anyone trading the S&P 500 should know that seasonal patterns are weaker there — and set correspondingly more conservative thresholds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the DAX more seasonal than the S&P 500?

Yes. The gap between the strongest and weakest months is wider in the DAX (approximately 4.2 percentage points) than in the S&P 500 (approximately 2.5 percentage points). Seasonal patterns are more pronounced and statistically more robust in the DAX.

### Why is September so weak in the DAX?

September is the weakest month in both indices. In the DAX, the effect is particularly strong at an average of -2.1%. Reasons include: position clean-ups after the summer break, hedging activity ahead of Q3 month-end, and lower liquidity due to European holiday seasons.

### Does Sell in May work better in the DAX than in the S&P 500?

Yes. The DAX return from May through October averages +0.4% per year, while November through April delivers +7.8%. For the S&P 500, the difference is smaller: +1.8% (summer) vs. +7.1% (winter). The "Sell in May" effect is approximately twice as strong in the DAX.
